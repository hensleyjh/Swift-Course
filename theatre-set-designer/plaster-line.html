How it works

1. Set up the theatre. Under Stage, pick proscenium, thrust or black box and enter the real sizes if you know them.

2. Add scenery. Under Add, select a piece to drop it center stage. Drag it in the ground plan. The white squares resize it and the round handle rotates it. In the front elevation, drag a piece up to set it on a platform, or drag the top handle to change its height.

3. Fine-tune. The right panel has exact measurements, colors, wall finishes, door swings and more. Positions are measured the way theatres do it: across from the center line and up from the plaster line.

4. Hand it in. Fill in the title block under Sheet, open the Drawing sheet view, then Export image. The sheet puts the ground plan above the front elevation at the same scale, so they line up.

Terms on the drawing

℄ Center line
Runs from downstage to upstage through the middle of the stage. Positions across the stage are measured from it.
PL Plaster line
The line at the upstage face of the proscenium wall. Positions up and down the stage are measured from it.
SR / SL
Stage right and stage left, from the actor's view facing the audience. Stage left is on the audience's right.
Upstage / downstage
Upstage is away from the audience; downstage is toward it.
Flat
A framed wall panel. Door, window and arch flats have openings.
Legs and borders
Black drapes that hide the wings (legs) and the lights overhead (borders).
Trim
The height of the bottom edge of a hanging piece above the stage floor.
Dashed lines
On a ground plan, long dashes mean the piece hangs overhead (flown).

Keyboard

Arrows
Nudge 6 inches. Hold Shift for 2 feet. In the elevation, up and down change height above the floor.
R / Shift+R
Rotate 15° clockwise / counterclockwise.
Ctrl+D
Duplicate the selected piece.
M
Mirror across the center line.
Delete
Remove the selected piece.
Ctrl+Z
Undo. Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes.
Esc
Deselect.
